## switch-01 turn 2 (topic_switch)
- **Follow-up:** How is the research desk's news score turned into a recommendation?
- **Searched:** How is the research desk's news score turned into a recommendation?
- **Expected:** research-desk/data/policy.md
- **Answered:** True | prompt tokens 4261
- **Answer:** The news score is combined with the price trend by a fixed rule, not by the model. Each counted article gets +1 if bullish for price, -1 if bearish, and 0 if mixed or unclear, and the news score is the sum of those values [2]. The recommendation then follows the policy: LONG if the trend is up and the news score is above zero; SHORT if the trend is down and the news score is below zero; otherwise NO_TRADE [2]. The trend is up above +1.0%, down below -1.0%, and flat otherwise, based on the 10-day percentage change from get_prices [2]. The policy states that Python owns the score summation and recommendation arithmetic, while the model may only interpret sentiment [2]. A test confirms this: it calls the score_news tool, reads the trend from get_prices, and checks that policy_recommendation(trend, news_score) returns the expected recommendation, such as NO_TRADE for a flat trend with a score of 0 [1].
- **Cited:** research-desk/data/policy.md :: Desk Research Policy; research-desk/tests/test_tools.py :: test_news_scores_and_recommendations_are_code_calculated
