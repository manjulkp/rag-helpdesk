## switch-01 turn 2 (topic_switch)
- **Follow-up:** How is the research desk's news score turned into a recommendation?
- **Searched:** How is the research desk's news score turned into a recommendation?
- **Expected:** research-desk/data/policy.md
- **Answered:** True | prompt tokens 4171
- **Answer:** The news score is combined with the price trend by fixed rules, and Python does the arithmetic.

1. Each counted article gets a score: +1 if bullish for price, -1 if bearish, and 0 if mixed or unclear. The news score is the sum of those scores. Only articles dated inside the research window and with source_type equal to wire are counted [2].
2. The trend comes from the 10-day percentage change returned by get_prices. It is "up" above +1.0%, "down" below -1.0%, and "flat" otherwise [2].
3. The recommendation rules are: LONG if the trend is up and the news score is above zero; SHORT if the trend is down and the news score is below zero; otherwise NO_TRADE [2].

The model may interpret sentiment, but Python owns the price arithmetic, source eligibility, score summation, and recommendation arithmetic [2]. A test checks this by calling policy_recommendation with the trend and news score and comparing the result to the expected recommendation [1].
- **Cited:** research-desk/data/policy.md :: Desk Research Policy; research-desk/tests/test_tools.py :: test_news_scores_and_recommendations_are_code_calculated
- **Grade:** correct (condense left the topic switch unchanged)
